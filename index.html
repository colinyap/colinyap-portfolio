Who I am, and how I ended up pointed at digital design.

# An engineer who likes the whole stack

I'm a Year 2 MEng (Hons) student at the University of Nottingham Malaysia, and I've spent it building across as much of engineering as I can reach — mechanical assemblies, embedded firmware, control systems, computer vision, and digital logic. The common thread isn't a single field; it's that I like taking something from a rough idea to a thing that actually works.

That's meant a PID-controlled line-following robot with computer vision, a wireless robotic hand driven by a flex-sensor glove, and a business card etched onto a PCB. Each one pulled me deeper into a different discipline — motor control, wireless comms, mechanical design, board layout.

Digital design is where that's settled. I've built a multicycle RV32I processor in Verilog, implementing the Zmmul multiply extension and a custom CRC instruction extension on top of the base ISA, and pushed it through synthesis on the open-source OpenLane flow. My aim is RTL design and design verification in Malaysia's semiconductor industry. I keep everything open on GitHub and write up what I learn as I go.

rvbl-2-riscv-core $ cat README.md
# RVBL-2 — RV32I Multicycle Processor

A complete RV32I_Zmmul_Xvbcrc processor in Verilog-2001: four-state control FSM, load-store unit, and a multiply unit. On top of base RV32I I implemented the Zmmul multiply extension and Xvbcrc, a custom CRC instruction extension in hardware. Verified with self-checking testbenches, then taken through synthesis on OpenLane with the sky130 PDK. Built for Phase 2 of the VombrenLabs ChampionCHIP competition.

isa= RV32I_Zmmul_Xvbcrc
microarchitecture= multicycle, 4-state FSM
extensions= Zmmul (multiply), Xvbcrc (CRC)
flow= OpenLane 1.1.1 / sky130A
standard_cells= 274,009

Silicon. The RVBL-2 core after place-and-route — the real GDSII layout that came out of the flow, rendered in 3D.

silicon/top.gds 11 MB · WebGL · 16 mask layers

What you're looking at

This is the same processor as the README above, but after synthesis and place-and-route — every logic gate resolved into physical rectangles of metal, polysilicon and diffusion on a sky130 die roughly a millimetre across.

The dense field is the standard-cell rows: 274,009 placed instances drawn from 1,277 distinct cells, each one an adder, a multiplexer, a flip-flop. The wide tracks running over the top are the routing layers carrying signal and power between them.
